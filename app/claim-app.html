Three steps: write the words, check them, add bitcoin. (Was this card given to you, with words already written on it? Go back and choose: This card was given to me.)

1

Get your 12 words

Tap the button and we'll create 12 words. Write them on the card's blank "your half" lines. Each tap makes a fresh set, so don't tap again after you've written them down.

2

Double-check what you wrote

Re-type the 12 words exactly as you wrote them on the card, in order, with a space between each word (extra spaces or capitals are fine). If they match, you know the card itself can unlock the bitcoin later.

3

Add bitcoin, seal, give

From your own wallet, send the amount you want to give to the address below. Then use the button underneath to check on it: once it says the payment is confirmed, seal the words and give the card.

How much to put on. A network fee comes off when the card is claimed, and that fee rises and falls with how busy bitcoin is: a few hundred sats when quiet, several thousand when busy. To be comfortable in any conditions, put at least 50,000 sats on the card. Below roughly 20,000 the card can be temporarily unclaimable during a busy spell. Nothing is lost when that happens, it just has to wait for fees to drop, or be claimed with the offline tool where the recipient picks the fee.
Tip for whoever you give it to: they scan the card, scratch the panel, and type both sets of words. No app, no sign-up.

Claim your bitcoin

Let's see what's on this card, then move it into a wallet of your own, one step at a time. Nothing moves until you tick a box and confirm at the final step, and you can start over at any point before that.

1

Enter the words from the card

You'll need both sets of 12 words from the back of the card, in the same order they appear on it. Type each set in order, with a space between each word (extra spaces or capitals are fine).

Before you scratch: do this on your own phone, somewhere private. Once the panel is off it stays off, and anyone who sees or photographs both sets of words can take the bitcoin.
2

Send it to your wallet

Open your bitcoin wallet app, tap Receive, and copy the address it shows into this box. It usually starts with bc1. An address is like an account number, not a password, so it is fine to copy and paste. Check it after pasting though: some phone and computer viruses swap a copied address for their own. No wallet yet? Tap the button below the box.

Is this app genuine? (optional check)
How the key is split. We make the 12 words under the scratch panel, so treat us as knowing that half. The other 12 words are made on your own device and we never see them. Both halves are needed to spend, so our half on its own is worth nothing, and that is the point of the design.
What the two servers see. Your words never leave your device. To check a balance and to send, this app talks to two public bitcoin servers (blockstream.info and mempool.space). They cannot move your bitcoin and never see your words or your name. They do see the card's address, your device's internet (IP) address, and, when you send, the wallet address you are sending to. Before this app will move anything, both servers must report the same balance: if they disagree or one is unreachable it refuses to sign rather than guess, so an outage at either one stops this app until it clears. Your bitcoin is unaffected by that, and the offline tool needs no servers at all.
Bitcoin payments are public and permanent. Anyone who knows this card's address, including whoever gave it to you, can look up which wallet you moved the money to and keep watching that address from then on. That is how bitcoin works everywhere, not something specific to this card. If that matters to you, send it onward inside your own wallet afterward.
Being straight about checking this app. The fingerprint below is worked out by this page itself, and the value you would compare it against is published on our site. If someone had replaced this page they could change both, so this catches an accident or a damaged copy, not a determined attacker. The check that actually holds is not depending on our website at all: the offline tool at bitcards.xyz/offline.html contains no network code and hands the key to a wallet app you already trust. For anything more than a small gift, that is the honest recommendation. Most people can skip all of this.
