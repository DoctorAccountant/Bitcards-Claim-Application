Three steps: write the words, check them, add bitcoin. (Was this card given to you, with words already written on it? Go back and choose: This card was given to me.)

Step 1

Get your 12 words

Tap the button and we'll create 12 words. Write them on the card's blank "your half" lines. Each tap makes a fresh set, so don't tap again after you've written them down.

Step 2

Double-check what you wrote

Re-type the 12 words exactly as you wrote them on the card, in order, with a space between each word (extra spaces or capitals are fine). If they match, you know the card itself can unlock the bitcoin later.

Step 3

Add bitcoin, seal, give

From your own wallet, send the amount you want to give to the address below. One honest note: a network fee comes off when the card is claimed, so very small amounts (a few thousand sats) can be mostly eaten by it. When the payment shows as complete, seal the words and give the card.

Tip for whoever you give it to: they scan the card, scratch the panel, and type both sets of words. No app, no sign-up.

Claim your bitcoin

Let's see what's on this card, then move it into a wallet of your own, one step at a time. Nothing moves until you tick a box and confirm at the final step, and you can start over at any point before that.

Step 1

Enter the words from the card

You'll need both sets of 12 words from the back of the card. Type each set in order, with a space between each word (extra spaces or capitals are fine).

Use your own phone in a private place. Anyone who sees or photographs these words can take the bitcoin.
Step 2

Send it to your wallet

Open any bitcoin wallet app you trust, tap Receive, and copy the address it shows into this box. It usually starts with bc1. An address is safe to copy and paste, it's like an account number, not a password.

You only need the 12 handwritten words. Keep the panel sealed. Type them in order, with a space between each word.

Is this app genuine? (optional check)
This app runs entirely on your device and your words never leave it. To look up balances and send payments it talks to two public bitcoin servers (blockstream.info and mempool.space). They are read-only windows onto the bitcoin network: they never see your words, never hold your bitcoin, and cannot change anything. The app also requires them to agree with each other before it trusts what they report. And if they ever shut down, any bitcoin tool can take their place, because your bitcoin lives on the bitcoin network itself, not with them and not with us.
