Three steps: write the words, check them, add bitcoin. (Was this card given to you, with words already written on it? Go back and choose: This card was given to me.)

Step 1

Get your 12 words

Tap the button and we'll create 12 words. Write them on the card's blank "your half" lines. Each tap makes a fresh set, so don't tap again after you've written them down.

Step 2

Double-check what you wrote

Re-type the 12 words exactly as you wrote them on the card, in order, with a space between each word (extra spaces or capitals are fine). If they match, you know the card itself can unlock the bitcoin later.

Step 3

Add bitcoin, seal, give

From your own wallet, send the amount you want to give to the address below. One honest note: a network fee comes off when the card is claimed, so very small amounts (a few thousand sats) can be mostly eaten by it. Then use the button below to check on it: once it says the payment is confirmed, seal the words and give the card.

Tip for whoever you give it to: they scan the card, scratch the panel, and type both sets of words. No app, no sign-up.

Enter the words from the card

Step 2

Send it to your wallet

Open any bitcoin wallet app you trust, tap Receive, and copy the address it shows into this box. It usually starts with bc1. An address is safe to copy and paste, it's like an account number, not a password.

Is this app genuine? (optional check)
This app runs entirely on your device and your words never leave it. To look up balances and send payments it talks to two public bitcoin servers (blockstream.info and mempool.space). They are read-only windows onto the bitcoin network: they never see your words or your name, never hold your bitcoin, and must agree with each other before this app trusts what they report. If they ever shut down, any bitcoin tool can take their place, because your bitcoin lives on the bitcoin network itself, not with them and not with us. What they do see is the card's bitcoin address and your device's internet (IP) address, so in principle a server could tell that the funding visit and the claim visit involved the same card. For most gifts this does not matter; if you want more privacy, move the bitcoin onward from your own wallet afterward.
Want proof you are running the real app? The fingerprint below is a hash of this app's program code and should match the value published at bitcards.xyz. For the whole file, save this page and compare its SHA-256 against the published file hash (for example, Get-FileHash on Windows, or shasum -a 256). The deepest guarantee is simpler: open this page only from the official address, because to send bitcoin it rebuilds the card's key on your device, so it trusts the page you loaded. Prefer not to rely on a website at all? Use the offline version at bitcards.xyz/offline.html, which hands the key to a wallet app you already trust. Most people can skip all of this.
